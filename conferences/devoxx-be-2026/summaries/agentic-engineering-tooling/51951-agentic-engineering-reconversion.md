# Agentic Engineering Reconversion
Victor Rentea · Keynote · Agentic Engineering & Tooling
Slides: https://victorrentea.ro/talk/agentic-reconversion-devoxx-26/

## TL;DR
Engineers must stop typing code and start directing agents, which demands faster learning, sharper system-design judgment, and deliberate protection of their own attention. Rentea frames this as a reconversion: new basics (skills, context, caching), new learning habits, and a closing list of forcing exercises to make the switch stick.

## Key takeaways
- Learn the agent basics first: context windows, custom skills, prompt caching, model and effort selection.
- Treat the agent as something to parent: feed it context and tools, then build feedback loops around the problem (he gives pixel-comparing two browsers during a JSP-to-Angular migration as the pattern).
- Memorize what the agent teaches with active recall: close the lid, explain it back, and re-test yourself the next morning.
- Running several agent terminals in parallel burns decision energy far faster than typing ever did, so learn your daily limit and your fastest way to recover.
- Generate over 95% of your code with AI by 2027 or fall into the bottom half of developers: his words, presented as the bar to clear.
- Never edit a project file by hand again; dictate prompts, describe the problem plus context instead of prescribing steps, and connect the agent to your tools instead of pasting text.

## Notes

### [00:33](https://youtu.be/vAfQ4f8wIVI?t=33) Setup: cloning himself and the plan
Rentea opens by saying he wrote code for 19 years and stopped six months ago. His own setup: slides generated by Claude, live transcription running locally, an SBX sandbox with network and filesystem rules, and a push-to-talk dictation rig built on a fine-tuned Whisper model. The plan: agent basics, what to do to your brain and mindset, then forcing exercises.

### [03:09](https://youtu.be/vAfQ4f8wIVI?t=189) Basics and agent parenting
The non-negotiable basics: context windows, writing skills (he points at Anthropic's Skill Jar as the lead to follow), prompt caching for cost control, and picking model plus effort by experiment. Then "agent parenting": give the agent context and tools, craft feedback loops, and be creative about verification, like handing it two browsers to click through old and new UIs side by side.

### [05:13](https://youtu.be/vAfQ4f8wIVI?t=313) Becoming an expert learner
Seniority that took a decade must now be rebuilt in months, so learning technique matters. From Make It Stick: active recall (close the lid, explain to the duck), spaced re-testing ("would you pass the exam the AI gives you on your own approvals?"), and chunking into groups of four. Slides carry at most four bullets as a live example.

### [06:46](https://youtu.be/vAfQ4f8wIVI?t=406) The new workday: decisions, terminals, burnout
The old rhythm (refinement, typing, Stack Overflow) is gone; the new one compresses three days of decisions into two hours across multiplying terminals, ending in a danger zone where you approve without reading. He names the costs: decision fatigue, drained agency at home, and burnout. Countermeasures: know your brain's daily limit, notice the blackout moment, and find the fastest detox that works for you (his: focus music, running, a named book on attention).

### [09:50](https://youtu.be/vAfQ4f8wIVI?t=590) What still belongs to humans
System design stays human work: boundaries, contracts, NFRs and trade-offs, data consistency, error handling. He suggests a cloud certification since infrastructure cannot be vibed. Manual debugging becomes analytical: Claude traced 17 memory leaks from a heap dump in 30 minutes (already shown at Voxxed Amsterdam), and a friend found a real bug in Keycloak after four hours of log digging. From there he tours mindset shifts: mental humility, critical thinking, escaping echo chambers, sensing when the model hallucinates, empathetic listening, and a product-owner stance as coding gets commoditized.

### [19:43](https://youtu.be/vAfQ4f8wIVI?t=1183) Agentic calisthenics
The closing checklist, shown in full on the final slide: AI-generate over 95% of your code by 2027; buy a personal Claude subscription and vibe a pet project; never hand-edit a file; dictate prompts for a whole day; vibe a complex feature from your phone; state problem plus context instead of instructions; multitask four parallel conversations; pair with an AI champion; connect agents to tools instead of pasting. He ends by saying superpowers need a quest: use them to help others or go insane.

## Tools, libraries and specs
- Claude (Anthropic; Opus, Sonnet, Haiku named) with agent skills and the Anthropic Skill Jar
- SBX sandbox (network and filesystem rules), free at time of talk
- Whisper Large V3 Turbo via MLX, fine-tuned on 40 hours of his voice (trained on a rented RunPod H100)
- WhisperFlow (dictation product, recommended by Karpathy and others per the speaker)
- VS Code / CLI over IntelliJ + Copilot, which he calls the worst AI combination
- iSAQB certification; AWS, GCP, Azure cloud architect certs
- Make It Stick (Brown, Roediger, McDaniel); Think Fast, Talk Smart (Stanford video)

## Who should watch
Engineers feeling behind on AI-assisted development who want a motivational map of what to change and a concrete exercise list. Skip when you already work agent-first daily: the techniques are named rather than taught, and the tooling advice is opinion, not comparison.

## Verdict
**5/10**: A motivational keynote carried by the speaker's own agent-first practice, limited to named techniques and baseline-free numbers.

**Reach broad**: Most of the conference audience codes with AI assistants daily and can try the agent-directing habits in their own work within the year.

**Novelty 1/2**

- **+** [02:07](https://youtu.be/vAfQ4f8wIVI?t=127) The voice-dictation run (Whisper Large V3 Turbo with a LoRA adapter on 40 hours of his own voice, trained on a rented H100) is his own unpublished experiment.
- **+** [05:13](https://youtu.be/vAfQ4f8wIVI?t=313) The learning habits (active recall, re-testing against the exam the AI would give on your own approvals, chunking into fours) are taken from Make It Stick and applied to reviewing agent output.
- **−** The basics stretch (context windows, skills, prompt caching) is Anthropic's getting-started material plus its Skill Jar showcase, which a search for the subject surfaces first.

**Demonstrated 1/2**

- **·** A 20-minute strategy keynote with no demo slot, whose mindset-and-habits subject has nothing central to run on screen.

**Measured 1/2**

- **+** [10:55](https://youtu.be/vAfQ4f8wIVI?t=655) The debugging story carries a count and a duration: 17 memory leaks from a heap dump plus 10 profiler bottlenecks in 30 minutes.
- **−** [10:55](https://youtu.be/vAfQ4f8wIVI?t=655) The 30-minute run has no manual baseline or described method, so the counts stay anecdotal.
- **−** [19:43](https://youtu.be/vAfQ4f8wIVI?t=1183) The 95%-by-2027 bar and the "up to 60 times more value" claim are asserted without a baseline or method.

**Depth 1/2**

- **+** [04:10](https://youtu.be/vAfQ4f8wIVI?t=250) The agent-parenting frame (feed the agent context and tools, build feedback loops around the problem) gives a beginner slot a working model for directing agents, with the two-browser migration check as the pattern.
- **−** [03:39](https://youtu.be/vAfQ4f8wIVI?t=219) Skills, prompt caching and model selection are assigned as homework ("You have to try it out") with no account of how any of them works.

**Reusable 1/2**

- **+** [20:16](https://youtu.be/vAfQ4f8wIVI?t=1216) The nine-item calisthenics list states each practice plainly enough to attempt week by week.
- **−** [20:16](https://youtu.be/vAfQ4f8wIVI?t=1216) The linked repositories cover the intro's dictation and review tools, and nothing published reproduces the exercise list itself.
